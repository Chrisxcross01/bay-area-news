Nvidia Wins Santa Clara Approval for HQ Expansion: What Nearby Owners Should Know

Fri Nov 20 2026

Nvidia has won approval from the City of Santa Clara to expand its headquarters campus, according to a November 19 report from The Business Journals. We could not read the full report or the city's approval documents. That means we have not confirmed the exact hearing date, the final project details or any conditions the city attached. Below, we explain what city records and earlier local reporting show about the expansion Nvidia has been pursuing, and what it could mean for people who own or are buying homes nearby.

Where the expansion is planned

For most of 2026, Nvidia's main pending expansion in Santa Clara has been a new building at 2300 and 2350 Walsh Avenue. That site sits directly across Walsh Avenue from the company's headquarters at 2788 San Tomas Expressway. The Silicon Valley Voice reported that San Tomas Aquino Creek borders the site on the west and a Caltrain station lies to the south.

Nvidia bought the business park on the 2300 block of Walsh Avenue, known as Santa Clara Commerce Park, for about $123 million in 2025, according to the San Francisco Standard. The Real Deal, citing the Silicon Valley Business Journal, reported that Nvidia filed for architectural review of the new building in December 2025.

What the city's project file describes

According to the City of Santa Clara's project listing (file number PLN25-00536), the application covers:

  • Demolition of 12 existing single-story office buildings
  • A new office/R&D building with three stories plus a mezzanine, totaling 692,634 gross square feet, at a floor area ratio of 1.0
  • A height exception (called a "minor modification") that would allow the building to reach 124 feet 11 inches where 100 feet is normally permitted
  • Parking in structured and surface lots. The original plan listed 2,137 spaces, and a later update listed 2,101.
  • A solar trellis over the surface parking
  • A pedestrian bridge over Walsh Avenue connecting to the existing campus at 2788 San Tomas Expressway

The Silicon Valley Voice reported that the building was designed by Gensler and would include a skylight, a metal facade and an undulating roof. If the approved version differs from these filings, the city's final approval documents are the authority.

How the approval process worked

A Santa Clara spokesperson told the Silicon Valley Voice in February 2026 that the project did not need a City Council vote. City staff would decide it at a public Development Review Hearing instead. The paper also reported that the city rezoned the area in 2024 to low-intensity office/research and development. That zoning allows corporate campuses and bans heavy industrial uses.

Nvidia held a community meeting on January 29, 2026, at Santa Clara City Hall and online. According to the city, it later held a follow-up meeting to update the plans in response to neighbors' feedback.

What neighbors said

Several residents at the January meeting raised concerns about the building's size, loss of privacy, construction dust and noise, and added traffic, the Silicon Valley Voice reported. One neighbor called the building "massive." Others spoke in favor, including a Walsh Avenue business park owner who welcomed Nvidia's further investment in the area. An Nvidia representative said construction would follow standard practices for noise, light and dust. The representative added that the company would work with the city on steps to reduce the impact on the surrounding neighborhood.

Timeline

Nvidia's team said the project would take 24 to 30 months to complete once approved, according to the Silicon Valley Voice. We have not seen a confirmed groundbreaking date. Demolition and building permits are usually separate steps that come after planning approval, so the actual start of work may come later.

This is not Nvidia's first recent expansion step in Santa Clara. Peninsula Press reported that the city approved the architectural review for a "Phase 3" of the headquarters expansion at a Development Review Hearing on October 1, 2025.

What this means for Bay Area homeowners, buyers and sellers

This is an office project, not housing, so it does not add homes to the market. Its effects on residents are more likely to come from construction, traffic and nearby job activity. Some practical points:

  • If you own near Walsh Avenue or San Tomas Expressway: Watch the city's project page for permit filings and construction notices. That will help you plan around dust, noise and lane closures, especially if you work from home or plan to sell during construction.
  • If you're selling nearby: Buyers often ask about large construction projects nearby. Ask your agent, and an attorney if needed, how to handle it in your disclosures.
  • If you're buying nearby: Visit at different times of day. Look up the approved plans for building height, the parking structures and the Walsh Avenue bridge so you know what will change around you.
  • If you're a small landlord: Large employer campuses can affect rental demand nearby, but we can't predict how this project will affect rents. A local property manager or lender can help you check your own numbers.

As details of the final approval become available from the City of Santa Clara, we'll keep watching how this project takes shape along Walsh Avenue.
