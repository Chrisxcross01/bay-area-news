Great America to Close After 2027: What It Means for Santa Clara Real Estate

Mon Nov 09 2026

This week's biggest Bay Area real estate story is in Santa Clara. Six Flags confirmed that California's Great America will close for good after its 2027 season, which makes one of Silicon Valley's largest single properties available for redevelopment. We're also following results from last week's election on several local ballot measures, and we'll cover them in a future update once we've checked the details against official results.

California's Great America Sets Its Final Day: September 6, 2027

On Friday, November 6, Six Flags Entertainment Corp. announced that California's Great America in Santa Clara will close permanently after one last "Farewell Season" in 2027. The San Francisco Chronicle and CBS News Bay Area both reported the news. The park plans to reopen in March 2027 and run through Labor Day, September 6, 2027, which will be its final day. CBS reported that Six Flags hasn't yet set an exact opening date. The company says it will release the full 2027 calendar later.

How we got here

The park opened in 1976 as Marriott's Great America. It sits along Great America Parkway in north Santa Clara, between Levi's Stadium and Mission College, according to KQED. The City of Santa Clara owned the land for decades and leased it to the park. KQED and San Jose Inside both report that the city had to sell the property after California dissolved local redevelopment agencies. San Jose Inside reported that Cedar Fair, the park's operator at the time, bought the land from the city in 2019 for $150 million.

In 2022, Cedar Fair sold the land underneath the park to Prologis, a San Francisco-based real estate company, for $310 million, according to the Chronicle. As part of that deal, Cedar Fair leased the land back so the park could keep operating. That lease runs until June 2028. Six Flags merged with Cedar Fair in 2024 and has now decided not to renew the lease. The Chronicle also noted that the announcement came after a hard year for Six Flags, which reported a $366 million net loss for the third quarter.

What happens during the final season

According to the Chronicle, Six Flags says the rides will keep running through 2027, subject to the usual maintenance, weather and operating conditions. The South Bay Shores water park is expected to follow its normal seasonal schedule. Group outings, corporate events and school and youth programs will continue through the final season. The park is selling 2027 Gold and Prestige passes with unlimited visits, and the Chronicle reports that those passes also cover Six Flags Discovery Kingdom in Vallejo.

What comes next for the 112-acre site

This is the part most Santa Clara homeowners will want to watch. In the coverage we reviewed, we didn't find a public redevelopment proposal for the property. When Prologis bought the land in 2022, San Jose Inside reported that the City of Santa Clara didn't yet know the new owner's plans. Commercial real estate experts quoted by The Mercury News at the time saw the land as a major redevelopment opportunity. They said the timeline could be anywhere from a few years to a decade.

Any large new use of the site would normally go through the City of Santa Clara's planning process. That process usually includes environmental review and public hearings before anything is approved. Until a formal application is filed, nobody knows how many homes, how much commercial space or what building heights the site might eventually have, so we suggest treating speculation with caution.

What this may mean for Bay Area homeowners, buyers and sellers

  • Nearby homeowners: Because no plan has been announced, it's too early to know how redevelopment might affect traffic, services or home values in north Santa Clara. You can follow City of Santa Clara planning agendas and sign up for city notices so you hear about any application on the site as soon as it's filed.
  • Buyers: If you're looking at homes near Great America Parkway, Levi's Stadium or Mission College, it's reasonable to factor in that this large site will probably change over the next several years. Ask your agent to help you look up public planning records for any parcel you're considering.
  • Sellers: There's no announced project to price in, positive or negative. We think it makes sense to price your home based on current comparable sales and not on guesses about what might happen on the site.
  • Small landlords: The park's final season runs through September 2027. Owners who rent near the park may want to keep an eye on any changes in local demand after it closes.
