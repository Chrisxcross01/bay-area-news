Mountain View Opens 85-Home Montecito Affordable Apartments Near North Shoreline

Sat Nov 14 2026

Mountain View has a new affordable housing community. On Thursday, Nov. 12, the City of Mountain View, the County of Santa Clara, the Santa Clara County Housing Authority, nonprofit developer Charities Housing and other partners held a grand opening for Montecito Multifamily at 1265 Montecito Ave. The City of Mountain View and Santa Clara County each announced the opening. The building has 85 apartments for households with very low incomes.

Below we cover what was built, how it was paid for, how long it took and what it means for people who live, rent or plan to buy in Mountain View.

Location and size

The building is a five-story apartment community at the corner of Montecito Avenue and North Shoreline Boulevard, in ZIP code 94043. Santa Clara County describes the site as about one acre near downtown Mountain View. According to earlier reporting by the Mountain View Voice, the new building replaced a vacant office building. The paper also reported that the surrounding blocks include a shopping center, other residential buildings and a car wash.

Here are the key details from Charities Housing and the Mountain View Voice:

  • Homes: 84 affordable apartments plus one on-site staff unit, for 85 in total.
  • Unit mix: 24 studios, 18 one-bedrooms, 21 two-bedrooms and 21 three-bedrooms.
  • Apartment sizes: Charities Housing lists studios at roughly 339 to 411 square feet, one-bedrooms at about 512 to 517 square feet, two-bedrooms at 668 to 824 square feet and three-bedrooms at 973 to 1,092 square feet.
  • Parking: Charities Housing lists 44 surface spaces. The site is within half a mile of major transit, so under the state density bonus law the project could provide less parking than usual. The Voice reported that the requirement was 0.5 spaces per unit.
  • Amenities: A community room with a shared kitchen, laundry, secure bike storage, a children's play area and outdoor barbecue space. The county also mentions a game room for teens.

Who the apartments are for

The city says the homes serve households earning between 30% and 50% of the Area Median Income (AMI). Half of the apartments, 42 units according to the county, are set aside for Rapid Rehousing. This county program, run through the Office of Supportive Housing, helps individuals and families move from homelessness into stable housing. Charities Housing says those residents can get temporary rental help, move-in support, education resources and job placement services. California's tax credit committee noted in a 2023 staff report that the project would also receive HUD Section 8 project-based vouchers.

Charities Housing posts the rental application, a flyer and a note that applications are reviewed in order of submission on its Montecito property page. Income and household-size rules apply. Anyone interested should read the application carefully or contact Charities Housing directly.

Timeline and funding

  1. December 2022: The Mountain View City Council unanimously approved the project at its Dec. 6 meeting, the Mountain View Voice reported.
  2. 2023: The Santa Clara County Board of Supervisors approved up to $18 million from the 2016 Measure A affordable housing bond for the project.
  3. Feb. 22, 2024: Construction began with a groundbreaking ceremony on the site.
  4. Nov. 12, 2026: Grand opening.

The county says its $18 million covered part of the construction and the purchase of the land. The city's grand-opening announcement says Mountain View committed $13.5 million. A 2024 Voice article put the city's commitment at $16 million, and we couldn't confirm what explains the difference. Other partners included Housing Trust Silicon Valley, which provided a loan to buy the land, and Destination: Home, which provided an early development loan. The Voice also reported that Wells Fargo joined as an equity investor.

Part of a larger pipeline in Mountain View

Montecito is one of several affordable projects Mountain View expects to finish or start this year. In May, the city said five developments scheduled to be completed or to begin construction in 2026 would add 553 affordable units. That would increase its affordable supply by nearly 30%. At the time, the city had 20 affordable housing projects with 2,044 units. The county says Montecito is one of six Mountain View developments in an agreement under which the county plans to provide $80 million. So far it has awarded about $62.6 million in bond funds toward more than 540 units.

What this means for homeowners, buyers and renters

  • For neighbors near North Shoreline: The building is finished, so construction on this site is over. If you live nearby, residents will be moving in over the coming months. Parking was discussed when the council approved the project in 2022. Questions about street parking or traffic should go to the city.
  • For buyers: Mountain View's housing supply is changing in many parts of the city. Before you make an offer, it's worth checking the city's list of approved and proposed projects near the homes you're considering, so you know what may be built nearby.
  • For renters and family members who may qualify: Eligibility depends on household income and size compared with the county's AMI limits. Charities Housing's application spells out the requirements.
  • For small landlords: Measure A and city funding are adding affordable units across Santa Clara County. If you rent to voucher holders or are thinking about it, the Santa Clara County Housing Authority can explain how its programs work.

We'll keep watching Mountain View's housing pipeline and report as other projects reach construction or open.
