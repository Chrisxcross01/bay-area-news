Santa Clara Approves Design of Nvidia's 690,000-Square-Foot Walsh Avenue Office

Sat Nov 21 2026

The City of Santa Clara has given Nvidia a key approval for the next big building at its headquarters campus. According to The Real Deal, which cited the Silicon Valley Business Journal, city officials approved the architectural review for the project at a development review hearing on Wednesday, Nov. 18. The new building is planned for a site on Walsh Avenue, across the street from Nvidia's hexagonal Voyager and Endeavor buildings on San Tomas Expressway. The Real Deal reports that the expansion is meant to hold thousands more employees.

Below is a summary of what's planned, where the project stands and why it matters to homeowners, buyers and sellers in Santa Clara and nearby cities.

Where the building would go

The City of Santa Clara's project listing puts the project at 2300 and 2350 Walsh Avenue. Nvidia's main campus sits along San Tomas Expressway between Central Expressway and Walsh Avenue. According to the Silicon Valley Voice, the new site is bordered by San Tomas Aquino Creek on the west and a Caltrain station on the south.

Nvidia bought the roughly 13-acre property in 2025 for $123 million in cash, according to SFGATE and The Real Deal. Those 2025 reports called it a 10-building campus and listed the addresses as 2348 and 2350 Walsh Avenue. The city's current listing says 12 single-story office buildings would be torn down. Those buildings total about 242,800 square feet.

Size, height and parking

Here is the project as the city describes it:

  • Building: one office building with three stories and a mezzanine. The city's latest description puts it at 692,634 gross square feet. An earlier version was 689,008 square feet, the figure The Real Deal used.
  • Height: the project includes a "minor modification" that allows a total height of about 125 feet (124 feet, 11 inches). The zoning normally allows 100 feet.
  • Parking: the city's listing describes three levels of underground parking with 2,050 spaces, plus surface parking under a solar-panel trellis. Elsewhere on the same page, the total is given as 2,101 spaces.
  • Connection: a pedestrian bridge over Walsh Avenue would link the building to the existing headquarters.

According to The Real Deal's December 2025 coverage, the building would echo the hexagonal shape of Voyager and Endeavor, and Gensler is the architect. At a February community meeting, Nvidia's team said it was aiming for LEED Gold green-building certification, according to a Citizen Portal summary of that meeting.

Approval stage and timeline

The project did not need a City Council vote. A city spokesperson told the Silicon Valley Voice that it would be decided by city staff at a public development review hearing. In 2024 the city rezoned the area to low-intensity office/research and development use. That zoning allows office campuses like this one and does not allow heavy industrial use.

Approval is not the last step. The Real Deal reports that Nvidia still needs building permits before work can start, and the project team said at the hearing that construction documents are still being prepared. Milpitas-based Devcon Construction is the builder, and San Francisco-based Ellis Partners is Nvidia's development partner. In February, the Silicon Valley Voice reported that construction would take 24 to 30 months once approved. No start date has been announced, so we don't know when the building would open.

This is the latest step in a longer expansion. According to Peninsula Press, Santa Clara approved the design of Nvidia's Phase 3 building in October 2025. That project is part of a headquarters plan the city first approved in 2008. At the hearing, Nvidia's director of global development said the company's commitment to Santa Clara had not changed despite recent leadership changes at Nvidia.

What neighbors said

Nvidia held community meetings on Jan. 29 and June 24, 2026, according to the city. At the earlier meeting, residents asked about building height, construction hours, dust, truck staging and traffic, and some asked for more renderings, according to the Citizen Portal summary. The Silicon Valley Voice reported that some residents also spoke in support of the project.

A related item: NASA studies a data center at Moffett Field

A few miles away, the Mountain View Voice reports that NASA has asked for a contractor to study whether land at Ames Research Center could be leased for a large data center. The center sits mostly in unincorporated Santa Clara County north of Mountain View. A NASA spokesperson said the study is advisory and doesn't commit the agency to anything. The same article notes that San Jose recently adopted a temporary moratorium on data centers.

What this may mean for Bay Area homeowners, buyers and sellers

  • Neighbors near Walsh Avenue and San Tomas Expressway: construction would likely last two years or more once permits are issued. You can keep up through the City of Santa Clara's project listings and watch for permit and construction notices.
  • Buyers: if you're looking at homes near the campus, think about possible construction activity and commute traffic, and ask the city about expected timing.
  • Sellers and owners: big employer projects take years to finish, and their effect on any one home is hard to predict. If you're deciding when to sell or refinance, a lender or tax professional can help with your own situation.

We'll keep watching this project as it moves toward permits and construction.
