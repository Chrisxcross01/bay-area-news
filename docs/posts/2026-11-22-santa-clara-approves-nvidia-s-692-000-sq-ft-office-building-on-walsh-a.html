Santa Clara Approves Nvidia's 692,000-Sq.-Ft. Office Building on Walsh Avenue

Sun Nov 22 2026

The City of Santa Clara has approved the design of Nvidia's next big headquarters building. It's a three-story office building of roughly 692,000 square feet at 2300 and 2350 Walsh Avenue, across the street from the company's main campus on San Tomas Expressway. City staff approved the project's architectural review at a Development Review Hearing on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, according to The Real Deal, which cited the Silicon Valley Business Journal, and Traded.

Here's what was approved, where the building will go and what comes next. We've also included some notes for homeowners, buyers and sellers in Santa Clara and nearby parts of San Jose.

Where the project is

Nvidia's main campus is built around its hexagonal Voyager and Endeavor buildings. They sit along San Tomas Expressway between Central Expressway and Walsh Avenue, according to The Real Deal. The new building will go up just across Walsh Avenue. The Silicon Valley Voice reported that San Tomas Aquino Creek borders the site on the west, with Caltrain to the south. In a post about the project, the city said a pedestrian bridge over Walsh Avenue would connect the new building to Nvidia's existing property at 2788 San Tomas Expressway.

According to the Silicon Valley Voice, Santa Clara rezoned the area in 2024 to low-intensity office and research-and-development use. That zoning allows campuses like this one and bans heavy industrial use. Traded reported that Nvidia bought the Walsh Avenue property in 2025 for about $123 million, which added about 13 acres next to its headquarters.

What will be built

The city's project page (file PLN25-00536) describes the plan this way:

  • Size: A three-story office building. The city's project description lists 692,634 square feet. The city's summary of the January community meeting lists 689,008 gross square feet, with a mezzanine and a floor area ratio of 0.99. The Real Deal also used the 689,008 figure. The two numbers likely reflect different ways of measuring, but the sources don't explain the gap.
  • Height: The application asks for a "Minor Modification" that would allow the building to reach 124 feet 11 inches, where 100 feet is normally permitted. The reports we read didn't say whether this request was decided separately at the hearing.
  • Parking: 2,050 spaces on three underground levels, plus a surface lot covered by a solar panel canopy.
  • Demolition: 12 one-story office buildings now on the site, totaling 242,802 square feet, would be torn down.
  • Workforce: Traded reported that the building is designed for more than 3,000 employees. It also called the project the fourth phase of Nvidia's headquarters expansion.

The Real Deal reported that Milpitas-based Devcon Construction is leading construction and San Francisco-based Ellis Partners is a development partner. Traded said the architect is Gensler.

How it got here and what's next

Nvidia held a hybrid community meeting at Santa Clara City Hall on January 29, 2026, and a follow-up meeting on June 24, 2026, according to the city and the Silicon Valley Voice. At the January meeting, some residents raised concerns about construction noise, dust and light. Nvidia's director of global development, Ryan Trinidade, said work would follow standard practices for those issues. He also said the company would work with the city on ways to reduce the impact on the neighborhood. A local business owner on Walsh Avenue spoke in favor of the project.

The project didn't need a City Council vote. Under the city's rules, staff decide architectural review at Development Review Hearings. According to the city's Architectural Review page, those decisions are final unless someone files a written appeal to the Planning Commission or City Council within seven calendar days. We couldn't confirm whether anyone has appealed.

Traded and The Real Deal both reported that Nvidia still needs building permits before work can start. We haven't seen a published construction start date or completion date.

What this may mean for local homeowners, buyers and sellers

This is an office project, and it doesn't include any housing. Even so, a large employer adding space for thousands of workers is worth watching if you own a home or are house-hunting in Santa Clara, west San Jose or the cities around them. A few practical thoughts:

  • If you live near Walsh Avenue or San Tomas Expressway: Demolition, digging three levels of underground parking and building the bridge over Walsh Avenue could mean months of construction traffic and noise once permits are issued. The city's project page is the best place to check for updates.
  • If you're buying nearby: Ask about planned construction during your due diligence, and try the commute at rush hour. Ask your agent to help you check the city's records for active projects close to any home you're considering.
  • If you're selling or renting out property: Job growth is one of many things that affect local housing demand. It's hard to predict how much one building will matter, especially since its timeline isn't set. If you're deciding when to sell or refinance, a lender or tax professional can help you weigh your own situation.
