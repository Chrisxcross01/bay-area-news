Santa Clara Approves Nvidia's 692,634-Square-Foot Office Building on Walsh Avenue

Wed Nov 25 2026

Santa Clara has approved a major expansion of Nvidia's headquarters campus. On November 18, 2026, city staff approved the architectural review for a new Nvidia campus building at 2300 and 2350 Walsh Avenue, according to a report from Traded. The Real Deal, citing the Silicon Valley Business Journal, also reported that the approval came at a development review hearing that Wednesday. The site sits directly across Walsh Avenue from the company's headquarters on San Tomas Expressway.

Whether you own a home near the site, are thinking about buying in Santa Clara, or simply drive San Tomas Expressway, here is what the city approved and what we know about the timeline.

What's being built

According to the City of Santa Clara's project page (file number PLN25-00536), the approved plan includes:

  • A three-story office and research building of 692,634 square feet. An earlier community meeting notice described it as three stories plus a mezzanine at 689,008 gross square feet, so you may see either figure in news coverage.
  • A height of 124 feet, 11 inches. The zoning allows 100 feet, so the city granted a "minor modification" for the extra height.
  • Three levels of underground parking with 2,050 spaces, plus surface parking under a solar (photovoltaic) trellis.
  • A pedestrian bridge over Walsh Avenue linking the new building to Nvidia's existing campus.

To make room, 12 single-story office buildings totaling 242,802 square feet will be demolished, the city says. San Francisco YIMBY reports that the lower floors will hold mostly research and development labs and that the top floor is planned as an event space with a work café and retail. The architect is Gensler, the firm behind Nvidia's hexagonal Endeavor and Voyager buildings across the street. The Real Deal reports that Milpitas-based Devcon Construction will build the project.

SF YIMBY estimates that the new garage will bring parking across Nvidia's whole campus to just under 5,000 spaces. Traded reports that the building is expected to hold more than 3,000 employees.

Where exactly is it?

The parcels (APNs 224-11-063 and -064) are near the corner of Walsh Avenue and San Tomas Expressway in ZIP code 95050. San Tomas Aquino Creek runs along the west side and the Caltrain tracks run to the south, according to SF YIMBY and the Silicon Valley Voice. Reports give different sizes for the property. SF YIMBY calls it roughly ten acres, while SFGATE and The Real Deal describe a 13-acre site. That's the parcel Nvidia bought for $123 million in cash in 2025.

When Nvidia bought it, the complex was leased to a mix of tenants, SFGATE reported. They included an Indian community center, a church, a fitness studio and a vocational school.

How it was approved, and what comes next

This project did not go to the City Council. A city spokesperson told the Silicon Valley Voice that council approval wasn't required and that city staff would decide at a public development review hearing. The Voice also reported that in 2024 the city rezoned the area to low-intensity office/R&D, which allows this kind of campus and prohibits heavy industrial uses.

Nvidia held a hybrid community meeting on January 29, 2026, and a follow-up meeting on June 24, 2026, according to the city. At the January meeting, some nearby residents objected to the building's size, possible privacy loss, construction noise and dust, and traffic. Others, including a Walsh Avenue business-park owner, spoke in favor. Nvidia's director of global development said the company would follow standard practices on noise, light and dust and would work with the city on ways to reduce impacts on the neighborhood, the Voice reported.

Approval doesn't mean construction starts right away. Traded notes that Nvidia still needs building permits. At the January meeting, the Silicon Valley Voice reported that construction was expected to take 24 to 30 months once it begins. We could not find a confirmed groundbreaking date.

What this means for Bay Area homeowners, buyers and sellers

  • If you live near Walsh Avenue and San Tomas Expressway: Expect demolition and then a construction period of roughly two years or more. The city's project page is the best place to follow updates, and the Planning Division can tell you how construction-hours rules and conditions of approval apply.
  • If you're buying near the site: Check the project page and drive the area at commute hours. A large new workplace and garage could change traffic patterns on Walsh Avenue, San Tomas Expressway and nearby streets.
  • If you're selling nearby: Buyers may ask about the project. Having the basic facts ready, like the approved size, height and expected construction length, can make those conversations easier. Your agent can help you think through any disclosure questions, and a real estate attorney can answer specific legal ones.
  • Small landlords: A building designed for more than 3,000 employees is worth keeping an eye on. Still, we'd caution against assuming any particular effect on rents or prices until the building is actually under construction.
