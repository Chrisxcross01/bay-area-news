Bay Area Transit Sales Tax Passes: What Changes in San Jose and the South Bay

Fri Nov 13 2026

Bay Area voters have approved the Regional Transit Measure, also called Measure RTM. It's a 14-year sales tax that will help pay for BART, Caltrain, VTA, Muni, AC Transit and several smaller transit agencies. Bay City News reported that the BART Board of Directors met on Thursday, Nov. 12, and heard a budget update that now includes money from the measure. The Metropolitan Transportation Commission (MTC) said that as of Monday's count, the measure was on track to pass with about 63 percent of the vote.

The measure needed a simple majority of all votes across Alameda, Contra Costa, San Francisco, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties combined. Early returns reported by Berkeleyside showed it ahead in every county. Santa Clara County's yes vote, at about 54 percent, was the lowest of the five.

What changes, and when

Under Senate Bill 63, the 2025 state law that allowed the measure, the tax is 0.5% in Alameda, Contra Costa, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties and 1% in San Francisco.

  • Start date: The San Francisco County Transportation Authority says collection begins April 1, 2027. Before that, the Public Transit Revenue Measure District, which is run by the MTC board, must certify the results and adopt the tax. KQED reported that this vote is planned for early December.
  • End date: The tax runs for 14 years. The Transportation Authority says it expires in 2041.
  • Local rates: KQED reported that Santa Clara County's countywide sales tax rate was 9.75% before the election. CBS News Bay Area reported that San Jose's combined rate will become 10.5%.
  • What it covers: According to KQED, the tax applies only to items that California sales tax already covers, such as clothing and furniture. In Santa Clara County, a $100 taxable purchase will cost about 50 cents more.

Where the money goes

The measure is expected to raise about $980 million a year, or roughly $1 billion. MTC's summary of SB 63 shows the following estimated shares for fiscal year 2031:

  • BART operations: about $330 million
  • Santa Clara Valley Transportation Authority (VTA): about $264 million
  • Muni: about $170 million
  • Caltrain: about $75 million
  • AC Transit: about $51 million
  • SamTrans: about $50 million

Other agencies have published slightly different near-term figures. For example, BART expects about $310 million a year starting in fiscal year 2028.

In the South Bay, VTA has more flexibility than most agencies. KQED reports that VTA can spend the money on a wider range of transit costs, not just operations. MTC's summary says that includes repaving streets that carry bus routes. A VTA spokesperson told KQED that the VTA board has committed not to use this money for the BART Silicon Valley extension into downtown San Jose. Recent reporting by KQED and The Mercury News says VTA is warning that the extension's cost and schedule are growing.

About $45 million a year will go through MTC to programs for riders. These include discounts when transferring between transit systems, a larger Clipper START low-income fare discount, and regional maps and signs. SB 63 also requires an independent oversight committee and a review of transit agencies' finances. It also bars agencies from using the new money to replace funding they already spend on operations.

What BART says comes next

Bay City News reported that BART had expected to close its 10 lowest-ridership stations as soon as January 2027 if the measure failed. BART General Manager Bob Powers told KTVU that those closures and service cuts are no longer planned. However, a BART statement quoted by CBS said the new revenue still leaves a yearly structural shortfall that BART has to close. BART's preliminary budget for fiscal years 2028 and 2029 is due in March 2027, and the board is scheduled to adopt the final budget in June 2027.

Opponents included the Silicon Valley Taxpayers Association and similar groups in other counties. According to CBS, they argued that transit agencies already receive billions of dollars a year and that the oversight rules are too weak.

What it means for South Bay homeowners, buyers and sellers

  • No new property tax line for South Bay owners. Measure RTM is a sales tax, so it won't appear on Santa Clara County property tax bills. San Francisco is different. Its voters also passed Proposition H, a parcel tax for Muni. The SFMTA says it will first appear on 2027–28 property tax bills, which are mailed in fall 2027.
  • Moving and furnishing costs. Furniture, appliances and other taxable goods bought after April 1, 2027 will cost a bit more. For example, a $3,000 appliance package bought in Santa Clara County would cost about $15 more. Buyers planning big purchases may want to consider that timing.
  • Commutes. Before the vote, campaign materials cited by KRON warned that Caltrain could have dropped to hourly service with no weekend trains. If you're comparing homes based on travel time, it now looks more likely that service at San Jose Diridon, Santa Clara, Sunnyvale, Mountain View, and BART's Milpitas and Berryessa/North San José stations will continue. BART's next budget will still show what service looks like after mid-2027.
  • Landlords and small business owners. If you also sell taxable goods, plan to update your sales tax rate on April 1, 2027. The California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA) publishes official rates for each location. A tax professional can help with questions about your situation.

We'll keep following the certification vote in December and BART's and VTA's budget decisions in 2027.
