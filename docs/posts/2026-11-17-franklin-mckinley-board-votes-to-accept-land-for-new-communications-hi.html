Franklin-McKinley Board Votes to Accept Land for New Communications Hill School

Tue Nov 17 2026

For decades, plans for San Jose's Communications Hill neighborhood have included a public school. On Nov. 10, the Franklin-McKinley School District board voted to move that plan forward. The vote was split, and the project still faces cost and deadline hurdles. Here is what we know, and what it may mean for homeowners, buyers and sellers in the area.

What the board decided

According to San José Spotlight, the Franklin-McKinley board voted 3-2 to accept a parcel of more than 7 acres from landowner MTA Hillsdale. The district plans to build a school there. Board President Hong Ha Hoang and Vice President Steven Sanchez voted no.

The school is estimated to cost about $93 million, San José Spotlight reported. The land comes with conditions and costs of its own:

  • Site preparation: The district would pay roughly $4.8 million to KB Home South Bay for grading, a clean-soil cap and moving a PG&E transmission line. Assistant Superintendent Jason Vann said the land would be worth about $20 million after that work.
  • A 2028 deadline: Superintendent Juan Cruz said MTA Hillsdale requires the district to commit $80 million toward construction by 2028. If it can't, the district loses the land.
  • Funding: The district plans to pay for the project with bond measures it has already passed and ones it may seek in the future.

Where Communications Hill is and how it has grown

According to the City of San José, Communications Hill sits on the hillside south of Downtown San Jose. It lies between Highway 87 and Monterey Road, north of Hillsdale Avenue and southeast of Curtner Avenue, in City Council District 7. The City Council approved the Communications Hill Specific Plan in 1992. The plan covers about 500 acres and calls for about 4,700 homes.

The city says about 2,500 of those homes have been built. The remaining phases include about 2,200 more homes, up to 67,500 square feet of commercial space and 1.44 million square feet of industrial park uses. The city's project page lists Phase 3 and Phase 4 permits as approved, with construction still to come. That includes a Village Center with 505 homes and 32,000 square feet of commercial space. The city also notes that a school is envisioned in the development but is not part of the current proposed project.

A city planning spokesperson told San José Spotlight that before issuing building permits, San Jose makes sure developers either pay school impact fees or meet their obligations by donating land or building facilities for school districts.

A long road to this point

The district has been working toward this site for years. According to the Franklin-McKinley project page:

  • The district hired an architect in 2018.
  • Voters passed Measure R in 2020, and the district says part of that bond money was earmarked for Communications Hill.
  • In April 2021, the board passed a resolution stating its intent to accept the donated land, as long as certain requirements were met.
  • The district sent its final Preliminary Environmental Assessment to the state Department of Toxic Substances Control in August 2022.

Why some trustees and teachers objected

The district's finances have been tight. In February 2025, San José Spotlight reported that the board voted to close Los Arboles Literacy & Technology Academy, Ramblewood Elementary and McKinley Elementary because of declining enrollment and a budget shortfall of almost $23 million.

Hoang told San José Spotlight that a lot has changed since the school was first planned more than 30 years ago. She pointed to falling enrollment, money problems and existing campuses that need upgrades. Leaders of the Franklin-McKinley Education Association, the teachers' union, also spoke against the project at the meeting. They argued that current schools need repairs first. They also said a new campus would likely shift students away from other district schools.

Trustee Rudy Rodriguez said he wants to keep a long-standing promise to build the school. He argued it would help the district keep students and add teaching jobs. Several Communications Hill residents also spoke in favor. One said a neighborhood school was part of why her family bought a home there in 2021.

What it means for local property owners

Franklin-McKinley property owners already pay for several voter-approved school measures. According to the district's Measure C page, the $142 million facilities bond on the June 2, 2026 ballot was estimated at about $30 per $100,000 of assessed value per year. San José Spotlight reports that voters approved it. Voters also approved Measure W in 2024, a $192 parcel tax that rises 3% a year. If the district pursues more bonds to pay for the new school, that would also go to the voters.

What buyers, sellers and homeowners can do

  • Buyers: A school site on a map or in a plan does not guarantee a school will open. In the sources we reviewed, the district has not announced an opening date, grade levels or attendance boundaries for the new campus. Check current school assignments directly with Franklin-McKinley's Enrollment Center before you rely on them.
  • Sellers: Describe the school as what it is today: a planned project that depends on funding and a 2028 commitment deadline.
  • Homeowners: Look at the school bond and parcel tax lines on your property tax bill. A tax professional can explain how these charges affect you.
  • Everyone: Future decisions on funding, design and boundaries will come before the Franklin-McKinley board at public meetings. Those meetings are the best place to follow the project and comment on it.
