Construction Begins on Canvas, 92 Affordable Apartments in Downtown Fremont

Wed Oct 28 2026

Work has started on a new affordable apartment building in Fremont's downtown. The project is called Canvas and sits at 39340 Fremont Boulevard in the city's Central District. Once it's finished, Fremont Boulevard will have 92 more homes in the downtown core. Construction is scheduled to wrap up in early 2028. San Francisco YIMBY, California Construction News and the Tri-City Voice all reported the start of construction in October 2026, based on a press release from the developer.

Below is what we know so far about the project and what it could mean for Fremont homeowners, buyers and renters.

Where it is

The City of Fremont's affordable housing office lists Canvas Apartments as a downtown project across the street from the Fremont Hub Shopping Center. The Tri-City Voice also places it near the Hub. The address is in the stretch of Fremont Boulevard that the city has been building up as its downtown. Several recent projects have added housing and mixed-use buildings to this area.

Project at a glance

  • Developer: Community Development Partners (CDP)
  • Size: A five-story building with 92 apartments, according to SF YIMBY and California Construction News
  • Unit mix: Studios and one-, two- and three-bedroom apartments
  • Affordability: Most apartments will be deed-restricted to households earning 30% to 70% of the area median income (AMI)
  • Units set aside: 15 apartments are reserved for households that include someone with an intellectual or developmental disability. These units come through partnerships with the California Department of Developmental Services and the Regional Center of the East Bay.
  • Design and construction: AO is the architect, and CDP Builders, the developer's own contracting arm, is building it
  • Estimated cost: About $59 million
  • Expected completion: March 2028. The City of Fremont expects leasing to start in spring 2028.

The city's listing says 91 units, while the news reports say 92. This is most likely because one apartment isn't income-restricted, often a unit for an on-site manager. SF YIMBY describes "most" of the units as deed-restricted, which fits that explanation. We weren't able to confirm the reason, though.

We also couldn't find the building's total square footage or height in feet in the sources we reviewed, so we've left those details out.

Amenities and resident services

California Construction News reports that Canvas will have two courtyards and a ground-floor community room with computers and reading areas. Other plans include rooftop solar panels to help power the common areas and a target of LEED certification.

Project Access will provide free resident services on site. These include homework help and tutoring after school, adult education, health and wellness classes, and community events. A resident services coordinator will help people sign up for benefits and connect with local resources. The developer says Canvas is meant to serve families, seniors and people with disabilities.

How it's being paid for

The main funding source is the federal Low-Income Housing Tax Credit program. California Construction News specifies a 4% tax credit allocation, with Bank of America as the equity investor. The City of Fremont is putting in $11 million and the California Department of Developmental Services is adding $1.5 million.

Why it matters for Fremont

Under Fremont's 2023–2031 Housing Element, the city needs to plan for almost 13,000 new homes, about 7,000 of them affordable, according to the Tri-City Voice and California Construction News. One 92-unit building won't close that gap. But projects like Canvas show how the city is using downtown sites and local housing money to work toward its state-required goals.

What this could mean for you

If you live or own property nearby

With construction lasting until early 2028, expect the usual signs of a building site along this part of Fremont Boulevard: crews, equipment and the occasional lane or sidewalk closure. Questions about construction hours or traffic should go to the City of Fremont.

If you're interested in renting at Canvas

Leasing hasn't started yet. The city's "Upcoming Affordable Units" page lists Canvas with a leasing date of spring 2028. Rents and income limits at income-restricted buildings are tied to AMI figures that change every year. Your eligibility will depend on your household size and income when applications open. It's a good idea to check the city's page now and then for updates.

If you're buying or selling in Fremont

Canvas is a rental building, so it won't add homes for sale. It's one more sign of the growth happening around Fremont's downtown and Central District. If you're thinking about buying or selling near the Hub or along Fremont Boulevard, it helps to know what's being built nearby and when it will open.
