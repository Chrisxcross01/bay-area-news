San Mateo-Foster City Schools' $498M Measure W: What Property Owners Should Know

Sun Oct 25 2026

If you own a home or a rental property in San Mateo or Foster City, you'll see a school bond on your November ballot. Measure W would let the San Mateo-Foster City School District issue up to $498 million in bonds to pay for campus upgrades and housing for teachers and staff. The district runs the TK–8 public schools in both cities. Below we cover what the measure would pay for, what it could add to your property tax bill and the key dates.

The basics: what's on the ballot and when

The district says its Board of Trustees adopted Resolution No. 06/25-26, which places the bond on the November 3, 2026 statewide general election ballot. The San Mateo County Elections Office lists it as "Measure W – San Mateo-Foster City School District: Bond Measure." It needs 55% of the votes cast on it to pass. The county's election page also links to the measure's full text, the tax rate statement, an impartial analysis and the arguments for and against. We suggest reading those before you vote.

What it would cost property owners

The district estimates the bond would cost property owners $29 per $100,000 of assessed value each year. The tax is based on the assessed value on your county tax bill, not on what your home would sell for today. A home with an assessed value of $1,000,000, for example, would pay about $290 a year. San Mateo County's elections office also lists other measures on the same ballot. One is Measure V, a separate bond for the San Mateo County Community College District.

Some points from the district's FAQ:

  • State law doesn't allow a senior exemption on school bonds of this kind, so the tax applies to all taxable property in the district.
  • Under Proposition 13, assessed values usually rise no more than 2% a year. That means longtime owners usually pay less than recent buyers.
  • The district says renters are exempt because the tax is charged to property owners.

Hoodline summarized reporting from the San Mateo Daily Journal on how this bond would stack with the district's existing ones. The bonds would be repaid over 30 years. Owners already pay Measure T, a $409 million bond approved in 2020, at about $30 per $100,000 through 2051. They also pay Measure X, a $148 million bond from 2015, at about $14.80 per $100,000, which is set to end by 2044. If Measure W passes, the three bonds together would come to about $73.80 per $100,000 of assessed value. Actual rates can change from year to year, so check your own tax bill for the exact figure.

The district also collects a separate parcel tax, Measure V from 2019, which is not the same as the college bond above. The Daily Journal reported in 2025 that this parcel tax is $349.40 per parcel a year and runs through 2028. It also reported that the district expects to seek a renewal.

Where the money would go

The district's bond page lists these estimated priority costs:

  • Workforce housing: $100 million
  • Replacing windowless classrooms: $84 million
  • Science, music and career technical education spaces: $69 million
  • Bowditch Middle School campus rebuild: $59 million
  • Classroom and site modernizations: $49 million
  • Furniture: $38 million
  • Painting: $31 million

Specific campus projects on the district's list include:

  • Science, music and career-education space at Abbott Middle School
  • Replacing classroom "pods" at Audubon Elementary and San Mateo Park Elementary
  • New science, music and library buildings at Bayside Academy and Borel Middle School

The list also covers fire alarms, roofing, window replacement and classroom technology. The district says many of its classrooms were built about 70 years ago. It also says many campuses have leaky roofs and fire alarms that are 25 years old. According to the Daily Journal, as reported by Hoodline, the district has identified more than $1 billion in total facility needs, with about $524 million considered urgent over the next five to 10 years.

Proposed staff housing at the Knolls Campus

For homeowners near 42nd Avenue in San Mateo, the housing plan may be the part to watch most closely. The district's workforce housing page says its Workforce Housing Leadership Subcommittee looked at two district-owned sites. One was the Knolls Campus at 525 42nd Ave., a former elementary school site. The other was the district office property on Chess Drive in Foster City. Early feasibility studies found room for about 80 units at either site. In February 2026, the subcommittee recommended pursuing housing only at the Knolls Campus.

This project is still at the concept stage. We found no approved site plan, building height or unit design yet. As reported by Hoodline, the Daily Journal says the housing would be among the first projects if Measure W passes. Using a design-build approach, district officials estimate it could be finished within about four years. The district says 608 employees answered its staff housing survey, and 96% said housing costs make it hard to recruit and keep staff.

The debate

Support for the bond isn't guaranteed. The Daily Journal reported that a 2025 district survey found about 57% of likely voters in favor, just above the 55% needed. Consultants said that was lower than in past polling. According to the Daily Journal's reporting as summarized by Hoodline, at least one resident has criticized the cost and the ongoing operating expenses. That resident suggested the district work with a private or nonprofit developer on the housing instead of owning it outright. The district points to its required safeguards: an independent citizens' oversight committee, annual audits, and a rule that no bond money can go to administrators' salaries or pensions.

What this means for homeowners, buyers and sellers

  • Homeowners: Find the assessed value on your San Mateo County tax bill and multiply by the estimated rate to see what the bond might cost you. Then read the county's voter materials before November 3.
  • Buyers: After a purchase, the assessed value resets to roughly the price you paid, so bond charges are based on that number. Ask your lender to include voter-approved taxes in your payment estimate, and check the "special assessments" and "voter-approved debt" lines on the seller's most recent tax bill.
  • Sellers: If Measure W passes, buyers and their agents will likely ask about the new charge, so it helps to have your current tax bill ready.
  • Small landlords: The tax is charged to owners. If you have questions about how it affects your rental's finances, talk with a tax professional.
