Construction Begins on 501 Apartments at Redwood City's ELCO Yards

Thu Oct 22 2026

The last big piece of the ELCO Yards redevelopment in downtown Redwood City is now under construction. According to an October 15, 2026 announcement from Trammell Crow Company, its residential arm, High Street Residential (HSR), and its joint venture partner Barings have broken ground on two apartment buildings. Together the buildings will hold 501 homes, and both are scheduled for completion in 2029.

This is one of the largest housing projects on the Peninsula right now. Below we cover what is being built, how the project has changed over the years, and what it may mean for people who own, rent or plan to buy near downtown Redwood City.

Where the new buildings are and what they include

According to the developer, the project is split between two addresses inside the ELCO Yards district:

  • 1555 El Camino Real: 252 apartments averaging about 751 square feet, plus 5,277 square feet of ground-floor retail.
  • 113 Beech Street: 249 apartments averaging about 787 square feet.

HSR says both buildings will offer studios through three-bedroom units, and some homes in each building will be set aside as affordable housing. The developer has not said how many. Planned amenities include rooftop decks and lounges, clubrooms, pools and spas, covered parking and coworking space. WRNS Studio, which also designed the completed commercial phase, designed both buildings. SBI Builders is the general contractor, according to REBusinessOnline.

On height, Redwood City Pulse reported that changes approved in November 2025 allowed one residential parcel, identified as Parcel A, to rise to seven stories within an 85-foot height limit. We could not confirm which of the two addresses matches Parcel A, or how tall the second building will be.

The bigger ELCO Yards picture

ELCO Yards was formerly called the South Main Mixed-Use project. It covers six blocks of downtown Redwood City, and Redwood City Pulse lists its address as 1601 El Camino Real. The city first approved the project in 2020, and work on the commercial buildings started in 2022, the Pulse reported.

According to Trammell Crow Company, the office and life sciences portion has been delivered and is about half leased. IQHQ led that portion. Reported tenants include the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, with about 224,000 square feet, and the law firm Orrick, with about 65,000 square feet. The full district is planned to include more than 600,000 square feet of office and lab space, along with childcare, restaurants, parks, plazas and public art. In its announcement, Barings pointed to the site's closeness to the Redwood City Caltrain station as a key reason for investing.

One detail doesn't line up across sources. The developer and REBusinessOnline describe ELCO Yards as having 501 apartments when finished. Earlier reporting by Redwood City Pulse on the city's approvals described 540 residential units, with nearly 150 of them affordable. We could not confirm from the sources we reviewed how those two figures fit together.

Affordable housing changes approved in 2025

Construction follows revisions that the Redwood City City Council approved in November 2025, according to Redwood City Pulse. The developer told the council that rising interest rates, inflation and other cost pressures made it hard to build a project designed before 2020 without changes. The Pulse reported that the approved changes:

  • Reduced the number of extremely low-, very low- and low-income units by 36 and increased market-rate units.
  • Added a housing impact fee of $5,822,001, where there had been no fee before.
  • Gave Redwood City residents, former residents and local workers preference for the units.
  • Made cost-saving design changes to materials such as panels, railings and windows.

The Pulse also reported that council members raised concerns about who will manage the buildings. At the time, several large property management firms were being considered.

What this may mean for Bay Area homeowners, buyers and renters

Nearby residents along El Camino Real and Beech Street, and in the blocks around downtown, can expect construction activity for the next few years. The City of Redwood City's Community Development Department is the place to go for construction notices and project documents.

Renters, including people interested in the affordable units, won't be able to apply until the buildings get close to opening in 2029. Since the units give preference to local residents and workers, it's worth watching for announcements from the city and the property manager about how and when to apply.

Small landlords who own rentals near downtown Redwood City should know that about 500 new apartments are scheduled to open around 2029. That much new supply near Caltrain could affect rent competition nearby. If you're thinking about pricing or refinancing a rental, a lender or tax professional can help you plan.

Buyers and sellers may find it helpful to follow how ELCO Yards fills in. The office space, the childcare facility and the apartments will all add activity to the area south of downtown. We'll keep tracking this project and other Peninsula and South Bay developments as they move forward.
