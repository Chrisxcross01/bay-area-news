Nov. 23 Roundup: Morgan Hill Flood Project, Moffett Data Center, Solar Tax Deadline

Tue Nov 24 2026

This week brought the finish line for a long-awaited flood protection project in South County and early signs that NASA may lease land at Moffett Field for a large data center. Homeowners thinking about rooftop solar also have about five weeks left before a long-running California property tax break ends for new systems.

Morgan Hill's Upper Llagas Creek flood project is finished

Valley Water, the City of Morgan Hill and Santa Clara County officials held a ribbon-cutting on Monday, November 16, to mark the completion of the Upper Llagas Creek Flood Protection Project, according to San José Spotlight and the Morgan Hill Times. The work covers 13.9 miles of improvements along Llagas Creek, West Little Llagas Creek and East Little Llagas Creek in Morgan Hill, Gilroy and unincorporated San Martin.

  • Who it protects: roughly 1,100 homes, 500 businesses and more than 1,300 acres of farmland, according to San José Spotlight.
  • Level of protection: the creek channels are designed to handle a 100-year flood, meaning a storm with a 1% chance of happening in any given year.
  • Cost: $395 million in total. More than $222 million came from Measure B, which Santa Clara County voters approved in 2012. Other sources included Valley Water, the California Department of Water Resources ($38.2 million) and Morgan Hill ($12.3 million). NBC Bay Area reported in 2024 that $80 million in federal money from the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service had been secured for the final phase.
  • Timeline: officials described it as an eight-year project, and Valley Water says the final phase began in September 2024.

These creeks have caused repeated flood damage since 1937, according to San José Spotlight, and officials welcomed the timing with a strong El Niño expected this winter.

What it could mean for homeowners: According to the Morgan Hill Times, city officials expect the project to take some Morgan Hill neighborhoods off FEMA flood maps. The reporting doesn't give a date for any map change or say which streets are included. If your home is in a mapped flood zone today, it makes sense to keep your coverage in place until FEMA officially revises the maps. The City of Morgan Hill or Valley Water can answer parcel questions, and your insurance agent or lender can explain how a map change would affect coverage requirements. Flood zone status also matters for sellers' disclosures and buyers' insurance quotes.

NASA studies leasing Moffett Field land for a data center

On October 15, NASA put out a request for quotes looking for a contractor to study whether land at its Ames Research Center could be leased for data center development, according to the Palo Alto Daily Post. Industry outlet Data Center Dynamics broke the story. It reported that the request refers to an Enhanced Use Lease and to hyperscale data center development.

This is a very early step. Data Center Dynamics says the contractor would look at available land, power supply and other feasibility questions. The Mountain View Voice reported that NASA hasn't said which sites it has in mind. A NASA spokesperson told the Voice the agency is studying several uses, including more supercomputing.

Ames covers about 2,000 acres, mostly in unincorporated Santa Clara County just north of Mountain View. Because the site is federal land, the Voice noted, Mountain View likely wouldn't have regulatory control over a data center there. Google already leases 42.2 acres at Moffett under a 40-year lease, according to the Daily Post. No size, power demand or timeline has been announced.

California's solar property tax exclusion ends for new systems after December 31

Since 1980, California has kept qualifying solar energy systems out of property tax reassessments, so adding panels hasn't raised a home's assessed value. That exclusion, found in Revenue and Taxation Code Section 73, is set to expire on January 1, 2027. Assessors around the state are warning property owners, including the Riverside County Assessor's Office and the San Luis Obispo County Assessor.

According to the San Luis Obispo County Assessor's notice:

  • Systems completed by January 1, 2027 keep the exclusion until the property changes ownership.
  • Systems still under construction on that date don't qualify and will be assessed as new construction.
  • Systems installed after the deadline will have their market value added to the property's assessment, which means a higher tax bill.

As Noozhawk explains, what counts is passing inspection with your local building department, not signing a contract. Noozhawk also reported that Assembly Bill 2389, which would have extended the exclusion through 2031, was held in the Assembly Appropriations Committee in May. The San Luis Obispo notice says the Legislature and Governor approved no extension.

What Bay Area homeowners can do: If you have a solar project underway in San Jose, Santa Clara or anywhere else in the region, ask your installer whether final inspection can realistically happen before year-end. Buyers looking at homes with solar may also want to ask how the system will be assessed after a sale. The Santa Clara County Assessor's Office can answer questions about specific properties, and a tax professional can help you weigh the numbers.
