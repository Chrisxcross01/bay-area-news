Bay Area Transit Taxes Passed: What the New Sales Tax and SF Parcel Tax Mean for You

Sun Nov 15 2026

Voters in five Bay Area counties have approved a new regional sales tax for BART, Caltrain, Muni, AC Transit, VTA and other transit systems. San Francisco voters also passed a separate Muni parcel tax that will appear on property tax bills. Here's what passed, when it starts and what it means for homeowners, buyers, sellers and small landlords.

What voters approved

The Regional Transit Measure (RTM), also called the Connect Bay Area Transit Initiative, was on the November 3, 2026 ballot in Alameda, Contra Costa, San Francisco, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties. A 2025 state law, Senate Bill 63, allowed it to go to voters. According to the Metropolitan Transportation Commission (MTC), it needed a simple majority of the combined vote in all five counties.

The measure adds a 0.5% sales tax in Alameda, Contra Costa, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties and a 1% sales tax in San Francisco, for 14 years. The San Francisco County Transportation Authority (SFCTA) estimates it will raise about $1 billion a year.

When the new sales tax starts

According to the SFCTA, collection begins April 1, 2027. KQED reports that the tax is set to end on April 1, 2041. Before collection starts, the Public Transit Revenue Measure District, which is governed by MTC's commissioners, has to certify the results and adopt the tax ordinance. KQED reports this is expected in early December.

KQED notes that the new tax applies only to items already subject to California sales tax, such as clothing, furniture and hot food. On a $100 taxable purchase, that works out to about 50 cents more in the four half-cent counties and $1 more in San Francisco.

Before the election, KQED reported these countywide rates: Alameda 10.25%, Santa Clara 9.75%, San Mateo 9.375% and Contra Costa 8.75%. Each will rise by half a point. San Francisco's rate goes from 8.625% to 9.625%. Many cities add their own local taxes on top, so your rate depends on where you shop. The California Department of Tax and Fee Administration publishes current rates by city.

Where the money goes

The money is meant mainly for day-to-day transit operations. According to the SFCTA, BART is expected to receive about $310 million a year, Caltrain about $70 million and AC Transit about $50 million. Estimates of Muni's share range from about $155 million (SFCTA) to about $170 million (KQED). About $45 million a year goes to programs for riders across the region, such as transfer discounts and the Clipper START low-income fare discount.

For the South Bay: According to KQED, SB 63 lets the Santa Clara Valley Transportation Authority (VTA) use money raised in Santa Clara County for "public transit expenses" in general, not only for operations. VTA told KQED that its board has committed not to spend RTM money on the BART Silicon Valley extension into downtown San Jose. KQED also reports that MTC staff expect the first payments to reach transit agencies by July 1, 2027.

San Francisco's Proposition H parcel tax

Proposition H is a 15-year parcel tax for Muni. According to the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency (SFMTA):

  • It first appears on the 2027–28 property tax bill, mailed in fall 2027, and is paid in the same two installments as regular property taxes.
  • Single-family parcels with up to 3,000 square feet of building area pay $129 in the first year. Larger homes pay more based on their square footage. A condo with its own parcel number is taxed as a single-family parcel.
  • Multifamily, commercial, mixed-use and vacant parcels have their own rate schedules.
  • Amounts rise with inflation starting in 2028–29, and the tax expires June 30, 2042.
  • Owners 65 and older who live in the property as their main home may qualify for an exemption, but they must apply. The deadline is June 1 for the upcoming tax year.
  • A lookup tool and forms for exemptions and corrections are scheduled to go live December 1, 2026.

For small landlords: SFMTA says owners of rent-controlled units can pass up to half the tax on to tenants, up to $65 per unit per year.

A related statewide result

CalMatters reports that California voters rejected Proposition 43. It would have required a two-thirds vote to pass local taxes set aside for specific services, so the current rules for approving these taxes stay in place.

What Bay Area homeowners, buyers and sellers may want to do

  • Buyers furnishing a new home may want to keep the April 1, 2027 start date in mind for large taxable purchases.
  • San Francisco buyers should add the Prop H parcel tax to their monthly cost estimates starting with the 2027–28 tax year. If you pay property taxes through an escrow account, ask your lender how the tax may affect your payment.
  • San Francisco owners 65 and older who qualify should apply for the exemption by June 1, 2027.
  • Landlords with questions about passing the tax on to tenants, or about taxes generally, may want to contact the San Francisco Rent Board or a tax professional.

Outside San Francisco, the regional measure is a sales tax, not a property tax. Homeowners in San Jose and the rest of Santa Clara, San Mateo, Alameda and Contra Costa counties won't see a new line on their property tax bills from it.
