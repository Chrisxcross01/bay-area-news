Amazon Hub in San Jose Reported Approved: What We Know About the South 7th Street Site

Thu Nov 19 2026

Hoodline published a story on November 19, 2026 under the headline "San Jose Amazon Hub Lands City Approval, Promises 350-Plus New Jobs." Before publishing this post, we couldn't read the full Hoodline article or find the city's approval record. That means we can't yet confirm which city body approved the project, when the decision was made, or what conditions came with it. The headline also doesn't name an address. The Amazon project that has been going through San José's planning process is a "last-mile" delivery hub at 1605 South 7th Street, so it is very likely the one Hoodline is reporting on. Below is what the City of San José's own filings and earlier news coverage say about that site.

Where the site is

The City of San José issued a Notice of Preparation for an Environmental Impact Report (EIR) in October 2024. According to that notice, the project sits on a 17.63-acre parcel at the southern corner of East Alma Avenue and South 7th Street. The city describes the location as central San José. The site has these neighbors:

  • North: East Alma Avenue
  • East: South 7th Street
  • South: a recycling center
  • West: the Sun Garden Shopping Center

The Real Deal, citing the Mercury News, places the property in the Spartan Keyes neighborhood just south of downtown and gives its size as 17.8 acres. The city says the land is designated and zoned Heavy Industrial, and that designation already allows warehouse and distribution uses.

The site is not empty land. According to the city, it has a vacant two-story industrial building of about 279,030 square feet and five smaller metal buildings, about 304,670 square feet in all. All of these buildings would be torn down, and 36 trees would be removed.

What Amazon proposed to build

The city's 2024 notice described the project this way:

  • A one-story concrete tilt-up warehouse of about 88,445 square feet, including about 8,676 square feet of office space
  • A maximum height of 46 feet, under the zone's 50-foot limit
  • Nine loading docks, with outdoor loading and unloading allowed around the clock
  • About 178 employee parking spaces and 413 delivery van spaces, plus electric-vehicle, bicycle and motorcycle parking

Later news coverage gave different numbers. In November 2025, The Real Deal, citing the Mercury News, described a 106,800-square-foot facility with about 8,700 square feet of office space and about 36,600 square feet of outdoor staging and loading area. The plans may have changed, or the reports may count the space differently. From the sources we reviewed, we couldn't tell which figures match the plans the city approved.

Jobs, trucks and hours

The job numbers also vary from source to source:

  • Hoodline's headline says more than 350 jobs.
  • The Real Deal's November 2025 summary of Mercury News reporting said 600 jobs.
  • The San Jose Blog, also citing that reporting, said no more than 400 employees would be on site at one time.

We don't know whether these numbers count different things, such as total jobs versus workers per shift.

Earlier reporting said the hub would run 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with about 52 truck trips per day bringing packages to the site. According to the San Jose Blog, vans would deliver to customers between 10 a.m. and 9:30 p.m. Filings described in the coverage suggest the hub would serve San José, Campbell, Los Gatos, Scotts Valley and Santa Cruz.

Timeline

In late 2025, reports said demolition would start in July 2026 and construction would finish in October 2027, with the whole project done by December 2027. We couldn't confirm whether that schedule still holds now that the project has reportedly been approved. Amazon bought the property about five years before that coverage for $59.3 million, according to The Real Deal.

What it may mean for nearby homeowners, buyers and sellers

  • Visit at different times of day. If you're buying or renting near South 7th Street, East Alma Avenue or the roads leading to them, go by early in the morning, in the evening and on a weekend. Loading is planned around the clock, and demolition and construction will add their own traffic for a while.
  • Read the environmental report. The city's EIR looks at traffic, noise, air quality and the steps required to reduce those effects. You can find it on the City of San José's Active EIRs page. The project's file numbers are H24-031 and ER24-153, and the Department of Planning, Building and Code Enforcement can answer questions about the final approved plans.
  • Sellers nearby: If you're getting ready to list a home close to the site, ask your agent whether you should mention the planned hub. For specific disclosure questions, a real estate attorney is the right person to ask.
  • Small landlords: A new employer can bring some extra demand for rentals nearby. But the job count and hiring schedule aren't settled yet, so it's best not to base pricing or buying decisions on this project alone.

Our take

This project would replace a large, empty industrial building on land already zoned for this kind of use. It doesn't require rezoning. For neighbors, the main things to watch are delivery traffic, nighttime activity and the construction schedule. We'll update this post once we can review the city's approval documents and confirm the final size, job count and timeline.
