Former Fry's in Fremont Begins Conversion Into Osgood Landing Manufacturing Hub

Tue Nov 24 2026

The former Fry's Electronics building at 43800 Osgood Road in Fremont is officially on its way to a second life. On Nov. 12, the Sterling Organization and the City of Fremont held a ceremonial "demo day" at the site, which is being reworked into an advanced manufacturing center called Osgood Landing, according to the Tri-City Voice. Mayor Raj Salwan and Joe Dykstra, president of Sterling Logistics Properties, spoke at the event. Afterward, city staff knocked down a wall as a symbolic start to the work.

Some headlines have called this a groundbreaking. It's more accurate to call it a conversion. The existing big-box building is being renovated for industrial use, not torn down and replaced. The Registry reported that the event also kicked off Sterling's search for tenants.

Where the project is

The 11.21-acre property sits near Osgood Road and Auto Mall Parkway, close to I-680, in the city's Irvington Community Plan Area, according to the city's state environmental filings. Fry's moved its Fremont store to this spot in 1999, and the chain closed all of its stores in 2021, the Tri-City Voice reported. A Sterling affiliate bought the property in June 2024 for $35.7 million, according to The Real Deal.

What's planned

The City of Fremont's environmental document describes the existing building as about 143,800 square feet and 37 feet tall. It also says the project would not change the building's height or footprint. Planned work includes:

  • Removing old retail interiors, replacing the roof and upgrading the building's electrical service
  • Adding an interior mezzanine of about 6,000 square feet
  • Up to 18 loading docks and 3 drive-in doors on the south side of the building
  • Outdoor storage on the southern part of the site for equipment, containers and trailers
  • Separate parking areas for trucks and cars, plus new paving, landscaping and façade updates

Sources give slightly different sizes. The city's filing uses about 143,815 square feet and Sterling's property page lists 143,795. The current leasing listing on LoopNet advertises 149,795 square feet, including about 12,000 square feet of office space on two floors. The larger figure may reflect the new mezzanine, but we couldn't confirm that.

According to The Real Deal, which cited the Silicon Valley Business Journal, Sterling expects to spend about $18 million on the renovation. Its total investment, including the land, is expected to come to nearly $65 million.

Approvals and timeline

  • March 2026: The city issued a Negative Declaration under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA). That's a finding that the project isn't expected to have a significant environmental impact.
  • June 9, 2026: The Fremont City Council voted unanimously to approve the project. The approval included changing the site's general plan designation from Regional Commercial to Tech Industrial, rezoning it from Commercial-Regional to Tech Industrial, and a design review permit. A second reading followed, with a notice filed with the state on July 7.
  • Nov. 12, 2026: Ceremonial "demo day" and the start of tenant marketing.
  • Completion: In June, Sterling said construction could begin this fall and wrap up next year, The Real Deal reported. The LoopNet listing shows a 2027 renovation date. We found no announced tenant.

Job estimates vary, too. The Registry reported the space could support up to 300 jobs, while the state environmental notice listed 50 employees. The real number will depend on who leases the building.

What it means for Bay Area homeowners, buyers and sellers

Osgood Landing won't add any homes. It is part of a bigger story about jobs in the southern East Bay, though. The Real Deal, citing CBRE data, reported that Fremont accounted for 75 percent of Silicon Valley's advanced manufacturing leasing last year. It also reported that Fremont and Newark together hold about 38 million square feet of industrial space. At demo day, Salwan said Fremont is "an international magnet for cutting-edge advanced manufacturing."

A few practical points:

  • Nearby residents: A manufacturing building with truck docks will likely bring different traffic than a retail store did, with fewer shoppers and more trucks. If you live near Osgood Road or Auto Mall Parkway, the city's environmental review page has the project documents. The city planner listed on the filings is Associate Planner Marc Cleveland.
  • Buyers: If you're looking at homes in Irvington or near the Auto Mall Parkway corridor, look over the site plan and drive the area on a weekday, so you can judge traffic and access for yourself. We haven't seen any data on how this project might affect home values, so be wary of anyone who says it will.
  • Sellers: Buyers often ask about nearby construction. It helps to know the basics: an existing building is being reused, its height isn't changing, and the use is industrial.

Osgood Landing is also part of a regional trend of reusing old Fry's sites. The Real Deal reported that Applied Materials bought the former Sunnyvale store site for $100 million. It also reported that Dividend Homes paid $67.7 million for part of the former Palo Alto Fry's property. If you own commercial property or a small rental near a large site like this and are weighing your options, a tax professional or real estate attorney can help you think them through.
