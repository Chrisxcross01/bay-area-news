BART Picks Developers for 643 Homes on Fremont Station Parking Lots

Tue Nov 03 2026

One of the biggest housing proposals in Fremont's City Center has moved forward. On October 22, 2026, the BART Board of Directors chose a team led by Pacific Development and Affirmed Housing to negotiate building homes on part of the surface parking at Fremont Station. The preliminary plan calls for 643 homes, and 176 of them would be affordable. BART also says the plan includes a landscaped plaza near the station entrance.

The vote doesn't approve the project. It starts a period of private talks between BART and the developers, and the City of Fremont's approval process comes after that. Here is what we know so far and what it could mean for Fremont homeowners, buyers and BART riders.

Where the project would go

BART calls this "TOD Phase 1," short for transit-oriented development. It covers about 8 acres on the northeast side of Fremont Station, at 2000 BART Way, close to Mowry Avenue. Right now the land is BART parking. The San Francisco Chronicle reported a larger figure of about 12 acres of parking lots. We use BART's 8-acre figure here because it appears in the agency's own project materials.

According to the Chronicle, many of the homes would look out over Tule Pond, a 17-acre wetland and stormwater system behind the station. BART says the plan is consistent with the City of Fremont's City Center Community Plan, which calls for this area to be mostly housing, with shops and services on the ground floor and possibly some public open space.

What's proposed

BART's project page lists these highlights from the team's preliminary site plan:

  • 643 new homes, including 176 affordable homes
  • A landscaped plaza near the BART entrance
  • New bicycle and pedestrian access from Mowry Avenue
  • A neighborhood café with play space and art studio space

The Real Deal, citing the Silicon Valley Business Journal, reports that the plan has two buildings. Pacific Development would build a seven-story building with 465 market-rate apartments. Affirmed Housing would build an eight-story affordable building with retail on the ground floor. According to a report on the board meeting published by Coastside News, the proposal also includes about 5,200 square feet of non-residential space. The developers would also pay for new streets and utilities on the site themselves.

BART plans to keep ownership of the land and lease it to the developers instead of selling it, according to The Real Deal. The design is preliminary, and the unit mix, building heights and layout could change during negotiations and city review.

Timeline and stage of approval

According to BART, the process so far has gone like this:

  1. Fall 2025: BART held open houses at the station to get community input.
  2. January 22, 2026: The board approved the project's goals and objectives.
  3. April 28, 2026: BART issued a request for proposals to developers.
  4. October 22, 2026: The board selected the developer team. The Registry reported that the vote was unanimous.

The next step is an exclusive negotiating agreement. Under that agreement, only this team can negotiate the deal with BART for a set period, and BART staff will bring the final terms back to the board later. Once the terms are settled, the project goes to the City of Fremont for entitlement, meaning land-use approval. The Real Deal reports that Ali Gaylord of Affirmed Housing expects the project to be fully entitled by 2028, and that construction on the market-rate building is planned to start first. No construction start or completion date has been confirmed yet.

What about BART parking?

Fremont Station commuters will want to know this one. BART says that once Phase 1 construction begins, at least about 700 parking spaces will stay available to riders in the station's other lots. In BART's fall 2025 count, 550 spaces were occupied across all of Fremont Station's lots. BART has also identified a possible second phase on another part of the station property.

The bigger picture

The Real Deal reports that Fremont's housing plan for the current cycle requires it to plan for 12,897 new homes by 2031, and more than half of those are expected to be affordable. The Fremont project is also part of a regional effort to build on BART parking lots. According to the Chronicle, 4,232 homes have been built on BART land so far, another 3,800 are in the pipeline, and 407 are under construction now. BART's request for proposals also notes that state transit-oriented housing laws, including AB 2923 and SB 79, shape what can be built on the site.

What this may mean for you

  • Nearby homeowners: If you live near Mowry Avenue or BART Way, you'll have chances to comment during the City of Fremont's review, likely starting no earlier than 2027 or 2028 based on the developers' timeline. You can sign up for BART's TOD updates through the agency's Profile Center to hear about meetings.
  • Buyers and renters: These homes are years away, so they won't change what's available to rent or buy in the near term. If you're interested in the affordable apartments, watch for announcements from Affirmed Housing and the City of Fremont closer to completion. Applications aren't open now.
  • Small landlords and sellers: Several hundred new apartments near the station could eventually add to the rental options in central Fremont. How much that affects local rents or home values will depend on timing and market conditions. If you're making investment or pricing decisions, a lender or tax professional can help you think them through.
  • BART riders: Keep an eye on BART's station access plan for Fremont. According to the request for proposals, the developer will pay for it, and it will look at how people get to the station by car, on foot, by bike and by bus.
