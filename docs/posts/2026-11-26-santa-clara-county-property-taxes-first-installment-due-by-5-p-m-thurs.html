Santa Clara County Property Taxes: First Installment Due by 5 p.m. Thursday, Dec. 10

Thu Nov 26 2026

If you own a home, condo or rental property anywhere in Santa Clara County, from Palo Alto and Mountain View to San Jose, Sunnyvale and Gilroy, the first installment of your 2026-27 secured property tax bill is due now. According to the county's Department of Tax and Collections (DTAC), you need to pay it, or get it postmarked by the U.S. Postal Service, by Thursday, December 10, 2026 to avoid a penalty. The county's notices say payments are due by 5 p.m. that day.

If you're a Palo Alto owner, or anyone else in the county, who still pays by check, one detail is easy to miss: the county asks you to write your parcel number and installment number on the check. Here's what the county's own materials say, and how to get your payment in on time.

The key dates

DTAC announced on October 1, 2026 that the 2026-27 secured bills, which cover July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027, were available to view, print and pay online. Based on the county's announcement and a 2026-27 bill posted on its payment portal:

  • First installment: due November 1, 2026; late after December 10, 2026.
  • Second installment: due February 1, 2027; late after April 10, 2027.

For each installment, the bill shows a higher "if not paid by" amount. That amount includes a 10% penalty and a $20 cost. A returned payment, such as a bounced check, adds an $85 fee.

Paying by check? Label it correctly

The payment coupons on 2026-27 bills ask you to write your Assessor's Parcel Number (APN) and the installment number (1 or 2) on your check. They also ask you to make the check payable to "SCC DTAC" and return the coupon in the envelope provided. The mailing address is 110 West Tasman Drive, San Jose, CA 95134. The county's October announcement uses the same "SCC DTAC" payee. Its mail-in payments web page lists the payee simply as "DTAC" and asks for your account, parcel or assessment number on the check. If in doubt, go by what your own bill says.

Your APN is printed near the top of your bill. If you own several properties, as many small landlords do, it helps to send a separate check and coupon for each parcel. That way each payment goes to the right bill.

What counts as "on time" by mail

The bill explains that mailed payments must be received, or postmarked at a USPS office, by the delinquency date. Postage-meter dates do not count as a USPS postmark. If your payment arrives after the deadline, you have to show proof that the Postal Service postmarked it on or before December 10. If you're mailing close to the deadline, think about getting a hand-stamped postmark at the post office counter and keeping a receipt. The bill also allows payment through approved private delivery services, using the date on the shipping label.

Other ways to pay

  • Online: You can pay on the county's portal at santaclaracounty.telleronline.net. The bill says there's no fee for an electronic check (e-check), while credit cards carry a convenience fee listed on the website.
  • In person: DTAC takes cash, checks and credit or debit cards at 110 West Tasman Drive in San Jose. Its website lists a 2.25% card fee, with a minimum of $2.50. The bill gives office hours of 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday.
  • Partial payments: The county says it accepts several partial payments toward an installment. This could help if paying the full amount at once is difficult.

Questions go to DTAC at (408) 808-7900. The bill lists phone hours of 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays.

What Bay Area owners may want to do this week

  1. Check who is paying. If your lender pays your property taxes through an impound or escrow account, check with your servicer before you send a payment yourself.
  2. Recent buyers, look up your parcel. If you closed on a home this year, check your APN on the county's payment portal so you know what's due and whether it has been paid.
  3. Look at your exemptions. The Santa Clara County Clerk of the Board's calendar lists December 10 as the last day to file a late homeowners' or veterans' exemption claim. If you live in the home and don't see the homeowners' exemption on your bill, contact the Assessor's Office. The bill also notes that some local agencies offer exemptions for seniors.
  4. Sign up for reminders. DTAC sends email notices about due dates at dtac.santaclaracounty.gov/notify.

If you're unsure how property tax payments affect your income taxes, a tax professional can help.

Own property in Alameda County too?

Alameda County's first installment has the same deadline: 5 p.m. on Thursday, December 10, 2026, according to the county's reminder bulletin. Payment methods are different, though. The Alameda County Treasurer-Tax Collector's FAQ says credit cards are accepted only online or by phone, not by mail or at the counter.
