Week of Nov. 16: BART Tunnel Milestone, San Jose Data Center Pause, Wood Stove Grants

Mon Nov 16 2026

This week brought a visible step forward for the long-planned BART extension through downtown San Jose and Santa Clara, along with a proposal at San Jose City Hall to hold off on approving new data centers until tougher rules are in place. Homeowners across the region also have a new chance to apply for grants that help pay to swap out wood-burning stoves and fireplaces for electric heat pumps.

Silicon Valley BART extension finishes tunnel launch shaft

According to San José Spotlight, crews working for the Santa Clara Valley Transportation Authority (VTA) have finished digging and reinforcing an 83-foot-deep shaft on a former rail yard near PayPal Park, on the San Jose–Santa Clara border. VTA identifies this area as the future Santa Clara Station and Newhall Yard site. The shaft is where a 53-foot-diameter tunnel boring machine will be lowered to start carving the roughly 5-mile tunnel for the new tracks.

Key details from the reporting:

  • Recent progress: Excavation wrapped up in September and concrete work for the underground structure finished in late October. The shaft connects to an 1,100-foot ramp that trains will eventually use to enter and leave the planned Santa Clara station.
  • Next steps: The boring machine still has to be shipped from Germany and reassembled. Tunneling is expected to start in early 2028, and VTA projects about 40 months to dig the full tunnel.
  • Stations and opening: The project would add four stations, three in San Jose and one in Santa Clara. VTA's project materials list stops including 28th Street/Little Portugal, downtown San Jose and Diridon. Passenger service is currently planned for 2037.
  • Cost questions: The project's budget stands at $12.7 billion, but San José Spotlight reports VTA has said it is not on track to stay within that figure and could consider changes such as delaying some stations until more money is found. A previously estimated funding gap of about $700 million is expected to be revised when VTA returns with updated cost figures in December.

What it means for you: If you own or are shopping for a home near the planned station areas or the Newhall Yard work site, this is a long-horizon project, and the scope may still change. It's worth watching VTA's December cost update before factoring station timing into a buying or selling decision.

San Jose weighs a short pause on data center approvals

Mayor Matt Mahan and Councilmembers Pamela Campos, David Cohen, Peter Ortiz and Domingo Candelas announced a proposal on Nov. 12 to temporarily stop land use approvals for data centers until new citywide standards are adopted and in effect, according to Local News Matters and San José Inside. The move followed reporting that a Google data center in Alviso could have been approved before the standards were finished.

As reported, the proposal would freeze final approvals at every level of city review, including director's hearings. KQED reports the pause would likely last only a few weeks. The group also wants an urgency ordinance so the new standards take effect as soon as the council approves them, rather than after the usual 30- or 60-day wait.

The draft standards, as described by KQED and other outlets, include carbon-neutral electricity, sewer monitoring, regular reporting of water and power use, and noise limits and monitoring for backup generators. Developers would also be encouraged to sign up with San Jose Clean Energy. A coalition of environmental and neighborhood groups, including Green Foothills, the Sierra Club Loma Prieta Chapter and Alviso in Action, is pushing for a longer moratorium to study cumulative impacts. Mahan has opposed an outright ban.

Key dates (as reported):

  • Nov. 17: City Council considers the temporary pause
  • Nov. 18: Planning Commission reviews the proposed standards
  • Dec. 8: City Council votes on the data center standards and community benefit requirements

Residents of Alviso, North San Jose and other areas near proposed sites who want to weigh in can follow the agendas on the City of San Jose's website.

Bay Area homeowners can apply for wood stove replacement grants

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District has opened a new round of its Clean HEET Program. The grants help cover the cost of replacing wood- or pellet-burning stoves, fireplace inserts and open-hearth fireplaces with electric heat pumps, which can both heat and cool a home. There's also a decommission-only option for removing open-hearth fireplaces or uncertified wood-burning devices.

  • Who can apply: Any homeowner in the Bay Area, according to the Air District.
  • How much: Grants range from $1,500 to $15,000 depending on the existing device and type of project, with an extra $10,000 available to eligible low-income households.
  • Deadline: 5 p.m. on March 10, 2027. Funds are awarded first-come, first-served.
  • Important: The program won't reimburse work that has already been done, so apply before you hire a contractor.

The program is funded by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's Targeted Airshed Grant Program, with matching dollars from the Air District. If you're planning to sell in the spring and were already thinking about updating an old fireplace, it may be worth checking eligibility now.
