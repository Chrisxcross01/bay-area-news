Palo Alto's Measure J Cubberley Sales Tax Holds Narrow Lead: What It Would Change

Wed Nov 11 2026

Measure J, Palo Alto's proposal to raise the city sales tax by half a cent, is still ahead in the November 3 election, though the margin is small. City leaders plan to use the money first to buy part of the Cubberley Community Center at 4000 Middlefield Road from the Palo Alto Unified School District (PAUSD). In returns posted Friday, November 6, the measure had 8,476 "yes" votes (51.43%) and 8,006 "no" votes (48.57%), according to the Palo Alto Daily Post. The measure needs a simple majority to pass.

These results are unofficial. The Palo Alto Ledger notes that mail ballots postmarked by Election Day could arrive through November 10 and still be counted. The outcome isn't final until Santa Clara County finishes canvassing and certifies the results. You can follow updates on the Santa Clara County Registrar of Voters results page.

What Measure J does

According to the City Attorney's impartial analysis, Measure J would add 0.5% to the transactions and use tax (the local sales tax) inside Palo Alto city limits. The city estimates it would raise about $15.6 million a year. Key points from the analysis:

  • Start date: If approved, the higher rate would take effect in April 2027.
  • No end date: The tax would stay in place until voters repeal it.
  • General tax: The money would go into the city's general fund and could be spent on any lawful city purpose. That is why it needed only a simple majority instead of two-thirds.
  • Nothing else changes: Which items are taxable, how the tax is collected and existing exemptions would stay the same.

The Daily Post reported that Palo Alto's sales tax rate was 9.75% before the election and would rise to 10.25% with Measure J. A separate regional transit sales tax on the same ballot would add another half-cent in Santa Clara County if voters approve it. California generally charges sales tax on tangible goods such as furniture and cars. Some groceries and prescription medicine are exempt, according to the same report. The city says state legislation, Senate Bill 762, gave Palo Alto the legal room to add the half-cent.

The Cubberley plan behind the measure

The Cubberley site covers 35 acres and opened as Cubberley High School in 1956, according to the City of Palo Alto. The city already owns eight acres there. It runs arts, culture and athletics programs and rents space at subsidized rates to nonprofit and community groups. Under an agreement with PAUSD, the city would buy seven more acres for $65.5 million. The city says the school district will put the sale money toward school improvements.

In June, the City Council adopted spending guidelines that name Cubberley as the top priority for Measure J revenue. The plan is to buy the land first and then start Phase 1 of the Cubberley Conceptual Master Plan. That would include updating older buildings, adding green space and expanding recreation and community space. The City Attorney's analysis points out that these guidelines are advisory and not legally binding. The Palo Alto Ledger, citing city project information, reports a preliminary Phase 1 cost estimate of $300 million to $400 million and says the scope could change. The city also expects to use partnerships, donations and development fees.

Why the vote was close

The campaign drew real debate. The Daily Post reported that the council first kept the 2024 land appraisals private. On October 13 it voted 7-0 to release them. The appraiser's first estimate was $60.2 million, and a second estimate after the city asked for more comparable sales came in at $43.6 million. City leaders said the $65.5 million price reflected the higher appraisal plus inflation. Opponents, including former councilmember Lydia Kou, objected to the lack of a sunset date and to the general-tax structure. Critics also noted that the ballot title, "Community Safety/Services Measure," doesn't mention the land purchase. Supporters included the full City Council and the PAUSD Board of Education, according to the Ledger.

What it means for Palo Alto homeowners, buyers and sellers

  • It's a sales tax, not a property tax. Measure J is a transactions and use tax. It is not a parcel tax or a bond, so the analysis gives no indication it would be added to property tax bills.
  • Bigger purchases would cost a little more starting in April 2027. If you plan to buy furniture, appliances or other taxable goods for a move or remodel, the extra half-cent is worth including in your budget. To check how a specific purchase would be taxed, ask a tax professional.
  • Cubberley is a long-term project. If the measure passes, the land purchase is just the first step. Phase 1 depends on funding beyond Measure J. Neighbors near Middlefield Road should expect years of planning, design review and public meetings before construction starts.
  • Stay involved. The city posts master plan updates on its "Help Us Improve Cubberley" page, and council agendas will show when spending decisions come up.
